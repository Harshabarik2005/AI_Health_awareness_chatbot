DENGUE FEVER - WHO/CDC Public Health Guidelines

Overview:
Dengue fever is a mosquito-borne viral infection caused by the dengue virus (DENV). It is transmitted primarily by the Aedes aegypti mosquito and, to a lesser extent, by Aedes albopictus. Dengue is prevalent in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, with an estimated 100–400 million infections occurring each year.

Symptoms:
Dengue fever typically presents 4–10 days after being bitten by an infected mosquito. Common symptoms include:
- High fever (40°C / 104°F)
- Severe headache
- Pain behind the eyes (retro-orbital pain)
- Muscle and joint pain (often called "breakbone fever")
- Nausea and vomiting
- Skin rash (appearing 2–5 days after fever onset)
- Mild bleeding (such as nose or gum bleeding, easy bruising)
- Fatigue

Severe Dengue (Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever):
Severe dengue is a potentially fatal complication. Warning signs typically appear 24–48 hours after the fever subsides and include:
- Severe abdominal pain
- Persistent vomiting
- Rapid breathing
- Bleeding gums or nose
- Blood in vomit or stool
- Extreme fatigue or restlessness
- Liver enlargement
- Fluid accumulation in the abdomen or lungs
Severe dengue requires immediate medical attention and hospitalization.

Prevention:
There is no specific antiviral treatment for dengue. Prevention focuses on:
1. Mosquito bite prevention: Use insect repellent (DEET, picaridin, or IR3535), wear long-sleeved clothing, and use mosquito nets.
2. Eliminate breeding sites: Remove standing water from containers, flower pots, tires, and other items that collect rainwater.
3. Community efforts: Support local vector control programs and ensure proper waste management.
4. Vaccination: The Dengvaxia (CYD-TDV) vaccine is approved for use in some countries for individuals aged 9–45 who have had a prior confirmed dengue infection. The TAK-003 (Qdenga) vaccine has been approved for broader use.

Treatment:
- Rest and adequate fluid intake
- Acetaminophen (paracetamol) for fever and pain relief
- Avoid aspirin and NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) as they increase bleeding risk
- Seek immediate medical care if warning signs of severe dengue appear
- Hospitalized patients may require intravenous fluid replacement and close monitoring

When to Seek Medical Help:
Seek immediate medical attention if you experience severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, difficulty breathing, bleeding, or extreme fatigue during or after a dengue infection.
