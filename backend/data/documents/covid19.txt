COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2) - WHO/CDC Public Health Guidelines

Overview:
COVID-19 is a respiratory illness caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus. First identified in December 2019 in Wuhan, China, it rapidly spread worldwide and was declared a pandemic by WHO in March 2020. The virus primarily spreads through respiratory droplets and aerosols when an infected person coughs, sneezes, talks, or breathes. As of 2025, COVID-19 continues to circulate with periodic waves driven by new variants.

Symptoms:
Symptoms can appear 2–14 days after exposure and range from mild to severe. Common symptoms include:
- Fever or chills
- Cough (usually dry)
- Shortness of breath or difficulty breathing
- Fatigue
- Muscle or body aches
- Headache
- New loss of taste or smell
- Sore throat
- Congestion or runny nose
- Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea

Severe COVID-19:
Risk factors for severe disease include advanced age (65+), obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, chronic lung disease, immunosuppression, and chronic kidney disease. Severe manifestations include:
- Pneumonia
- Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)
- Multi-organ failure
- Blood clotting disorders
- Long COVID (Post-Acute Sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 / PASC)

Long COVID:
Some individuals experience persistent symptoms for weeks or months after the acute infection resolves. These may include:
- Chronic fatigue
- Brain fog and cognitive difficulties
- Shortness of breath
- Joint or muscle pain
- Heart palpitations
- Sleep disturbances
- Depression or anxiety

Prevention:
1. Vaccination: COVID-19 vaccines remain the most effective tool for preventing severe disease, hospitalization, and death. Updated vaccines targeting current variants are recommended annually, similar to influenza vaccines.
2. Masking: Wearing well-fitting masks (N95, KN95, or surgical masks) in crowded or poorly ventilated indoor spaces reduces transmission.
3. Hand hygiene: Wash hands frequently with soap and water for at least 20 seconds or use hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol.
4. Ventilation: Improve indoor air quality by opening windows, using air purifiers with HEPA filters, and ensuring proper ventilation systems.
5. Testing and isolation: Test if symptomatic or after known exposure. Isolate if positive to prevent spreading to others.
6. Physical distancing: Maintain distance from others, especially during outbreaks or in high-risk settings.

Treatment:
- Mild cases: Rest, hydration, and over-the-counter medications for symptom relief (acetaminophen, ibuprofen).
- Antiviral medications: Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) is recommended for high-risk individuals within 5 days of symptom onset to reduce the risk of severe disease.
- Monoclonal antibodies: Availability varies based on circulating variants; check current CDC/WHO guidance.
- Severe cases: Hospitalized patients may require supplemental oxygen, dexamethasone (corticosteroid), remdesivir (antiviral), and supportive intensive care.
- Long COVID management: Multidisciplinary rehabilitation addressing specific symptoms (pulmonary, cognitive, cardiac).

When to Seek Medical Help:
Seek emergency medical care if you experience difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain or pressure, confusion, inability to stay awake, or bluish lips or face. High-risk individuals should contact their healthcare provider promptly after a positive test.

Source: World Health Organization (WHO), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
